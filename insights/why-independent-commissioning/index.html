Insight · Commissioning

Why independent commissioning matters.

Complex systems need an objective verification process that follows the owner’s requirements across design, construction, testing and operation.

Commissioning is most valuable when it is treated as an independent quality process rather than a late-stage testing activity.

The core problem: fragmented responsibility

Designers, contractors, vendors and operators each hold part of the information needed to demonstrate performance. Their scopes are often contractually separate, while the asset must operate as one connected system. Independent commissioning creates a common verification structure across those boundaries.

What independence changes

An independent role can test assumptions without being responsible for defending the original design or installation. That does not replace the responsibilities of designers and contractors. It gives the owner a separate line of evidence focused on requirements, interfaces, performance and operational consequence.

The objective is not to find more comments. It is to establish whether the asset can perform as required, and to make any remaining risk explicit.

Commissioning starts before testing

The strongest programmes begin by defining owner requirements, acceptance criteria, system boundaries and responsibilities. Design and commissionability reviews then identify problems while they are still easier to resolve. Installation verification, functional testing and integrated scenarios build on that foundation.

Evidence should support a decision

Test records need context: approved procedures, safe prerequisites, calibrated instruments, witnessed results, exceptions, corrective action and retest evidence. A passed test without traceable conditions may not provide the confidence an owner or operator needs.

The operating team is part of the process

Operators should influence requirements, test scenarios, training and handover. Their participation helps connect project evidence to real procedures, alarms, response plans and maintenance needs.

Questions to ask before appointment

  • Is the commissioning role independent from design and installation responsibility?
  • Does the scope begin early enough to influence requirements and commissionability?
  • Are integrated and failure-mode scenarios included?
  • How are issues classified, verified and closed?
  • What evidence will support the final readiness decision?
