Quality management

Quality is planned, not inspected: the case for ITPs

An Inspection and Test Plan turns an intention to do good work into a specific, checkable sequence.

You cannot inspect quality into a finished structure. By the time a defect is visible at the end, it is buried, expensive and often unfixable.

An ITP maps every work activity to its acceptance criteria, test method and frequency, and the points where work must stop for inspection before it can proceed.

Hold points make the control real

A hold point that can be passed without inspection is just a suggestion. A genuine hold point stops the work until quality is confirmed — precisely when defects are cheapest to fix.

We build ITPs against the specification before work starts, so quality is designed into the sequence rather than chased at the end.
