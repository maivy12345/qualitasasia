02 / Assure

Quality evidence you can defend.

Focus quality controls on the systems and failure modes that matter, with traceable inspection, test and closeout records.

The need

Completion is not the same as conformity.

A risk-led assurance process checks not only whether work is finished, but whether it meets requirements and can be proven through reliable records.

Quality planningITP reviewSite surveillanceNCR controlTurnover records
Scope

Quality controls across the supply chain.

Assurance intensity follows technical criticality, delivery risk and acceptance needs.

01

Quality planning

Review project quality plans, inspection and test plans, hold points and acceptance evidence.

02

Vendor and factory assurance

Support supplier reviews, manufacturing surveillance and FAT readiness or witnessing.

03

Installation surveillance

Inspect critical works, preserve observations and confirm corrective action.

04

Non-conformance control

Assess impact, root cause, disposition and verified closeout of quality issues.

05

Turnover dossier assurance

Check that handover records are complete, coherent and aligned with installed assets.

Typical outputs

Quality status with context.

Reporting distinguishes document completeness from actual technical confidence.

A

Assurance plan

Risk-based review and surveillance activities aligned to programme milestones.

B

Findings register

Clear classification, evidence, responsibility, due date and closure status.

C

Quality readiness report

A concise view of conformity, outstanding risk and required decisions.

When to engage

Intervene where defects become expensive.

  • Critical equipment is entering manufacture or factory test.
  • Installation quality varies across packages or contractors.
  • Non-conformances are accumulating without robust closure.
  • Handover records do not reflect actual installed status.
  • The owner needs independent confidence before acceptance.
